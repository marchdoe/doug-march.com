# Creative Brief — doug-march.com
**Date:** May 5, 2026 | **Signal Influence: 5/10 (Moderate)**

---

## Mood

Late spring Tuesday: the mind turning inward while the day stretches longest. There's a quiet competence in the air—multiple languages shipping, engineering problems being solved, craft being refined—but it's offset by a waning moon and a Tigers loss that lands like a small, honest disappointment. Hold both: make the design feel *productive and slightly deflated at once*. The site should breathe (13.8 hours of daylight demand spaciousness) but with a subtle weight beneath—not melancholy, but grounded. Spring's fresh saturation tempers any heaviness.

---

## Composition Direction

**Scroll with Index sensibility.**

Today's signal mix—multiple technical threads (Rust, TypeScript, Python layered across GitHub), competing Awwwards design directions, a leaderboard-style golf result—demands a compositional strategy that *sequences information horizontally/vertically without forced hierarchy*. The Scroll archetype respects the Tuesday weekday energy (linear, purposeful) while Index's visual restraint prevents the design from feeling either chaotic or aggressively curated. Let elements reveal themselves in sequence, not all at once.

---

## Typography Direction

Type should be **generous in vertical space but restrained in character**. Deploy a single clear sans-serif at multiple scales—avoid fussy detailing. Headings should *whisper authority*, not shout; use scale and spacing to create hierarchy, not weight. The waning moon and golf loss suggest a design that favors clarity over drama. Pair this with tight, precise letter-spacing (nod to the Guided by Voices + Rust-forward GitHub activity) so individual elements feel intentional, almost surveilled. No soft roundness—edges should be readable.

- **Tigers loss (4-5, May 4) → Single-line scoreline placed asymmetrically in the footer or margin.** Tone: matter-of-fact, even dry. No celebration badge. The loss is acknowledged as data, not drama. Keeps the slight weight/deflation without dominating.

- **Golf leaderboard (Cameron Young -19) → Display as a compact, left-aligned list element with only top 3 positions.** Treat it as ambient information—quietly impressive, like a leaderboard in a quiet clubhouse. Don't make it a feature; make it texture. This respects the hacker/builder energy without turning the site into a code dump.

Don't reference them directly. Instead, borrow their DNA: *craft an experience that feels bespoke and intentional (Kindred), interactive but not showy (Shader), and globally aware (WFEO). Avoid heavy ornamentation.*

- **Ming-Dao Deng quote ("mind turned inward, still-point of peace") → Potential footer element or subtle typographic anchor.** If the designer finds resonance with today's deflated-but-grounded mood, this quote can live as a small, centered typographic moment—but only if it feels earned, not forced. Use judgment.

- **Mother's Day (5 days away) → Visual nod only. No thematic takeover.** A single soft accent color or subtle seasonal element (May florals as texture, not imagery) can hint at the approaching holiday. Restraint is key.

## Palette Direction

**Spring greens and warm grays, low saturation contrast.** The season demands fresh color, but the waning moon and Tuesday's structured energy call for restraint. Avoid the full saturation of peak spring—dial saturation back by 15-20%. Introduce a warm, almost taupe gray as a grounding neutral, paired with a single spring green (muted, not neon) for accents. The Tigers loss and inward quote suggest the palette should feel *composed*, not energetic. Contrast should be clean but not high—warm light backgrounds, warm-toned darks. No cool grays. Keep the overall feeling like a clear May afternoon filtered through slightly aged glass.
