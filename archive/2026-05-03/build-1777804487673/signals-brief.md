## Mood

A late-spring Sunday afternoon when the light is still generous (13.7 hours of daylight) but the day already feels like it's winding down. The energy is **spacious and unhurried** — weekend permission — but there's an undercurrent of intellectual curiosity threading through (strong HN and GitHub signals, design references all pointing toward *build-it-yourself* platforms and innovation). Hold the tension between **relaxation and restlessness**: a designer on Sunday scrolling through Awwwards and thinking about shipping code. The moon is nearly full (waning gibbous at 94.5% illumination), lending subtle **dramatic contrast** to everything it touches — don't flatten this; let it create visual drama even in the spaciousness.

## Composition Direction

**Gallery Wall with Scroll undertones.**

Today's signals pull in multiple directions at once — Awwwards references show distinct design languages (shaders, engineering, youth culture), GitHub suggests technical depth across languages, and the quote + music + sports create a personal collage. Gallery Wall lets you hold these disparate elements in conversation without forcing false hierarchy or narrative. It respects Sunday's permission to browse, not sprint. The Scroll undertone means each "wall" item should feel discoverable, not all-at-once — there's motion, but it's leisurely.

---

## Typography Direction

**Generous spacing with theatrical contrast.** 

Type should breathe — this is weekend reading energy, not Monday morning density. But pair that generosity with **high-contrast weight jumps** (the near-full moon demands visual drama). Headings should have personality without shouting; they should feel like they're *inviting* exploration rather than commanding it. Use angular precision in accent typography (inspired by the Radiohead + technical GitHub signals) but keep body copy warm and approachable. Let negative space do as much work as letterforms.

## Palette Direction

**Spring-forward and luminous with minimal saturation.** Favor bright off-whites, pale creams, and cool fresh greens — the palette of mid-May light before the heat saturates everything. Keep the saturation low to mid-range; this is not a candy palette. High contrast between light and dark (driven by the near-full moon signal) means your darks can be near-black and your lights can be nearly white, but avoid muddy mid-tones. Temperature should lean **slightly warm** (spring has turned; it's not early-April coolness) but not sun-baked. The feeling is: *clarity*, not *warmth*. Think of the specific quality of 6 PM light on a May Sunday in the Midwest — clear, directional, without harshness.
